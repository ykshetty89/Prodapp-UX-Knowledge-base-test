### Driver Role in Pre-loading — Preserved Driving Hours
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #driver #preloading #driving-hours #trondheim #workflow

Terminals with long-distance routes require hub workers to pre-load trucks so drivers can focus solely on driving. Today, drivers do manual loading themselves, consuming their legal driving-hours allowance. At Trondheim, pre-loading is already practiced — night shift pre-loads the entire day's distribution and drivers stay in car during loading at smaller terminals.

### Driver App vs Hubworker — Two-App Role Split
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #driver #hubworker #driver-app #mental-switching #role-split #cognitive-load

"Hubworker is filling 9 out, Driver uses it when driving." Workers using Hubworker during loading AND being expected to interpret Driver App logic creates mental switching confusion. Workers in the hub worker role are functionally acting as a driver during confirmation steps. This two-app split is a core source of cognitive load in the current workflow.

### Driver Departure — Route Optimisation Timing Gap
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #driver #route-optimisation #departure #glow #open-question

Route optimisation may happen post-loading, after the driver has departed. The exact trigger point for route optimisation — before or after HW confirms the load — is an open question. This directly affects whether the HW's loading order matters at all, and must be resolved before designing the confirmation and sorting flows.
