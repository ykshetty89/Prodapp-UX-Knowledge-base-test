### Non-Scannable Goods — Highest Friction Point in Loading Workflow
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #warehouse #non-scannable #manual-entry #friction #glow #error-prone

Non-scannable items (pallets with letters, flyers, empty roller cages) must be manually identified, counted, and entered by hub workers with no system prompting. There is no visual distinction in the UI between scannable and non-scannable tasks. The process is entirely dependent on worker memory and dispatcher communication, making omission and miscounting the most common error class in the loading workflow.

### Non-Scannable Goods — Data Exists in Glow But Not Surfaced
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #warehouse #non-scannable #glow #display-gap #prioritisation #mvp

Non-scannable items are always pre-planned — the system knows they exist in Glow — but are never surfaced to the worker proactively in Hubworker. This is a display and prioritisation gap, not a data gap. Making non-scannable items visible and explicitly distinguished in the task list would eliminate a major error class without requiring backend changes.

### Zebra Device Constraints — Single-Hand Scan-First Interaction
- **Date:** 01 Jun 2026
- **Author:** Prodapp UX team
- **Source:** PDF — Hub Worker Loading UX Research Synthesis
- **Tags:** #warehouse #zebra #device #single-hand #scan-first #gloves

Handheld Zebra scanner has a small screen and works well for linear scan flows but struggles with complex interactions, multi-panel layouts, or dense information. Workers are in motion on the terminal floor, often wearing gloves. UI interactions must be minimal: large tap targets, clear visual feedback, no multi-step forms mid-task. Design must be optimised for single-hand, scan-first operation.
